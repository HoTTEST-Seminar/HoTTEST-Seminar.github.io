Title: What it takes to verify a kernel and a new way to prove it
YouTube: https://youtu.be/7BhFr80qC68
Slides:
Abstract: Dependent type theory is maybe the most expressive logic currently available. It is particularly good at letting its users directly and naturally express their mathematical ideas, without going through complicated encodings. However, this comes at a cost for those who build proof assistants based on type theory: constructing a kernel, the part of the system dedicated to checking a proof's correctness, is not an easy task! In this talk I'll give an overview of the difficulties one faces when trying to verify the correctness of such a kernel, in particular how I have come to view a set of properties I call *definitional inversions* as particularly critical. I'll also show a new approach, via domain theory, to establish these properties.
